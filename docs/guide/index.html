1Before you start

  • A Model:Cycles and a USB cable that carries data (some cables only charge).
  • Chrome, Edge or Opera on a computer. Firefox and Safari have no Web MIDI: there the page offers the .syx to download, for Elektron Transfer.
  • The official OS file model-cycles_OS1.13.syx, from elektron.se, unzipped.
  • Your projects backed up with Elektron Transfer.

The Samples OS tab (on this page of the flasher) needs one more file, the official model-samples_OS1.13.syx. The SAMPLE machine needs only the Model:Cycles file.

No firmware is provided here. The page modifies your own file on your computer and checks the result against a reference build before sending it.

2Flashing

  1. In the flasher, pick the cards you want: machines, effects, Track FX and mods, and they combine. Details on a card gives its full description and a link to its page in this guide. Then press Install under the cards.
  2. Drop your OS file. The firmware is built and checked in the page.
  3. Close Elektron Transfer, connect the Model:Cycles over USB and turn it on normally: no menu to open. Allow MIDI in the browser: the page finds the Model:Cycles and shows its OS version.
  4. Flash: about 30 seconds. Then confirm the update on the Model:Cycles screen with YES. It writes the firmware and restarts by itself: don't turn it off before.

The page uses the update protocol of Elektron Transfer, worked out by Elektroid: the Model:Cycles checks every block as it arrives. You can also download the prepared .syx in step 4 and drop it onto Elektron Transfer's window.

If the page doesn't find the Model:Cycles, download the .syx in step 4 and install it with Elektron Transfer: just as fast.

Keep the flasher tab in the foreground during the transfer: browsers slow down background tabs.

3SAMPLE

By durchgemacht · MIT license

One extra machine, SAMPLE, plays a sample from the +Drive once per trig or in a loop, forward or reverse, the way the Model:Samples does: 6-tap interpolation at 96 kHz, then the Model:Cycles' own decimator and amp chain, and a DJ-style filter modelled on the filter of the Model:Samples. The sample is chosen in the PRESET browser and saved with the sound, so projects, kits, presets and sound locks keep it. It is the Cycles One-Shot machine, built here from its own code at version 0.15.

  1. Put samples on the +Drive first: with Elektron Transfer, or with the Uploader in Chrome, Edge or Opera, which works with the official OS too. Samples left there by the Model:Samples OS work as they are.
  2. Press MACHINES and pick SAMPLE, the 7th machine, after Chord. Its icon is Tone's.
  3. Open the PRESET browser (FUNC + MACHINE) on that track and open a sample file: the sound takes the sample's name and plays it. Hold a step while you open a sample and the step gets it as a sound lock.
  4. Shape the sound with the four knobs (table below). DECAY starts at 127 so a sample plays out; GATE is heard with a lower DECAY; PUNCH works as on Tone. PITCH, the keys and FINE set the pitch as on every machine: PITCH 0 at note 60 plays the sample as recorded, whatever its sample rate.
  5. Hold MACHINES and press PUNCH to switch Loop on or off, GATE for Reverse. On a SAMPLE track MACHINES works as a held key: the machine list opens when you let go of MACHINES without having pressed PUNCH or GATE, so the screen keeps showing the sample while you switch. While MACHINES is held, PUNCH's and GATE's LEDs show the two switches instead of PUNCH and GATE. Loop plays from Start to the end again and again with a hard seam, as on the Model:Samples, so a single-cycle waveform loops without a step; the note then sounds until DECAY closes it (with GATE: until the note ends, then DECAY). Switched off while it plays, it runs to the end and fades out. Reverse plays from the end back to Start, from the next trig on; with both on it loops backwards. Both are saved with the sound.
  6. Hold one or more steps and MACHINES, then press PUNCH or GATE to lock Loop or Reverse on those steps: they play it the other way round from the track, saved with the pattern as a parameter lock. Press it again and they follow the track again. While you hold a step, the right half of the screen shows STEP with its number and LOOP and REV, lit when that step plays them (with several steps held, the first one, and + after its number); with MACHINES held too, PUNCH's and GATE's LEDs show the same. Hold a step and press MACHINES alone to open the PRESET browser: it opens when you let go of MACHINES.
  7. Start and Length also act on the note that plays. Turn SHAPE, or let an LFO move it, and the note ends where Length now puts it, with its short fade-out; COLOR waits for the next trig, so an LFO on Start still gives each trig a start of its own. On a loop, SHAPE moves the end at once and COLOR moves where the next time round starts, as on the Model:Samples: the loop wanders with it, with the same hard seam each time round.
  8. The main screen shows a narrow column on the left: the current pattern's number, T1 to T6 for the track with its pan under it (the number on the left when panned left, on the right when panned right, 0 in the middle), and the track's level as a bar that moves while you turn LEVEL. FUNC + LEVEL turns the pan 2 a step, as on every track; on a SAMPLE track a pan on an odd value, such as 63 at the right end, lands on the even value next to it, so 0 can always be reached. To its right the sample's name sits in a box, ending with L and R while Loop and Reverse are on, the waveform below it; two lines mark where a trig starts and stops, and move as you turn COLOR and SHAPE.
  9. Hold a trig and turn COLOR or SHAPE to lock its Start or Length: the whole screen shows the waveform for that trig, at full width, the two lines on its locks (the sound's own Start or Length where it has none), and with a sample lock that sample and its name. With several trigs held, it shows the first with a lock of that knob or a sample lock. The trig's note, velocity and length come back once the value disappears; holding a trig without turning shows them on the left and the step's LOOP and REV on the right.

Sound
one voice per track, once per trig or in a loop. No clicks at the edges: 4 ms fades at the end of what a trig plays, and at its start when Start lies inside the sample; a trig that cuts a sounding note brings the new one in over 0.67 ms, as on the Model:Samples. The filter fades rather than switches across the centre, so a square LFO on Cutoff does not click; at Cutoff 0 the track is untouched, bit for bit. Stereo files play as mono; files at other rates keep their pitch.
Samples
the sample memory is 47 MB of RAM the Model:Cycles' OS leaves unused. Samples load in the background when a project, kit or sound names them, and unload when nothing does.
Limits
Loop and Reverse are switches: they can be locked per step, but cannot be an LFO destination. The filter runs before Volume + Dist, where the Model:Samples has it after: with DIST above 0, the distortion acts on the filtered signal.
Other mods
goes with CHORD Tables and the sequencer, screen, MIDI and USB mods. Not with MACRO, ACID, the Syntakt engines, Model-TG, the per-track filter, sample preview or LFO 2, 3, 4 for now: the other machines take the same place in the firmware and there is no combined build with SAMPLE yet; Model-TG and the per-track filter change the same sound record functions; LFO 2, 3, 4 keeps LFO 2 where SAMPLE saves Loop and Reverse.

4CHORD Tables

model27's own · MIT license

CHORD plays your own wavetables from the +Drive. Each pattern chooses one, and every CHORD track in the pattern plays it; SWEEP moves through its waveforms as through CHORD's own.

  1. Make your wavetables on the Wavetables page. Fill its 31 slots, then press Save…. Under CHORD Tables, choose the wavetable's number and press Save .wav.
  2. From another program, a wavetable is up to 32 single-cycle waveforms of 256 samples, one after the other, mono, at 48 kHz. Its first waveform should be a sine; if it is not, CHORD Tables puts one in front.
  3. Put them on the +Drive with the Uploader, in a folder Wavetables at the top. Start each name with the wavetable's number, two digits from 01 to 63, then a name if you like: 07 Glass Pad.wav is wavetable 7, and so is 07.wav. The Uploader drops the .wav, and it warns you when CHORD Tables will not play a file as you named it.
  4. Press MACHINES and pick CHORD. Press and hold PATTERN, then turn SWEEP. The popup shows Wavetable and a number, one number every two steps of the knob: 0 is CHORD's own sound, 1 to 63 the file with that number.
  5. Turn SWEEP to move through the wavetable. At the far left you hear a sine. To let the LFO move through it, press and hold LFO, then turn SWEEP. SWEEP becomes the LFO's destination.
  6. Save the project. Each pattern keeps its wavetable.
Patterns
one wavetable per pattern, for every CHORD track in it. When the pattern changes, the new wavetable comes in at once, also on notes that still sound, without a click.
Files
a wavetable uploaded, deleted or renamed while the Model:Cycles runs is read within about two seconds. A number without a file plays CHORD's own sound. A name that does not start with 01 to 63 is not played (7 Pad, 070). Of two files with the same number, the one whose name comes first alphabetically plays, capitals before small letters.
Load
the wavetable plays from the same fast memory as CHORD's own, so six CHORD tracks with LFOs on SWEEP cost about what they cost with CHORD's own sound.
Other firmware
without CHORD Tables, another model27 build or the official one, every pattern plays CHORD's own sound. Flash CHORD Tables again and the patterns play their wavetables again. If you saved the project in between, set the wavetables again.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, the effects, the Track FX, the CPU Page, RESAMPLE, Transpose, Trig Condition Order, Trig Preview and Control All Config.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code: every CHORD track sounds sample for sample like the stock OS switched to the same wavetable, through every change of pattern, and like the stock OS at 0. Tested on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-08), from the command line with all fourteen other cards: the knob, the wavetables, SWEEP and LFOs, six CHORD tracks with LFOs and Control All, pattern changes, saving and loading, the other mods, files added and deleted, and the official OS. The names after the number are checked in the emulator and tested on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-09) with the same fifteen cards: a name after the number, a name without one, two files with one number, a deleted file, and six CHORD tracks under load; then flashed from this page.

5Digital Delay

Based on PPPCCC · MIT license

A second delay for the delay slot: the opposite of the dark stock delay, bright and clean, up to 5.4 seconds, each repeat a little thinner in the lows. Each pattern chooses its delay.

  1. Turn up a track's Delay Send, as for the stock delay.
  2. Hold PATTERN and turn Delay Time to the right: after four detents the pattern switches to Digital, and the two delay knobs read Digi Time and Digi Fdbk. Four detents to the left switch it back to the stock Delay.
  3. Delay Time and FUNC + Delay Time (Fdbk) work as usual: Time follows the tempo up to 5.4 seconds, and Fdbk at 127 keeps the repeats going almost endlessly.
  4. Save the project: each pattern keeps its own choice, and changing pattern changes the delay with it.
Sound
mono: the two sides of the send summed, the same repeat on both. A high-pass of about 240 Hz and a low-pass of about 12.8 kHz sit in the feedback. It starts from silence each time it is chosen: no old echo comes back.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, POTS and Control All Config. On a firmware without Digital, the official one too, a pattern that chose it plays the stock Delay.
Tested
its sound is PPPCCC's bit for bit, checked in an emulator against their own code. On a Model:Cycles (2026-10-27), from the command line, then flashed from this page alone and with SAMPLE: the choice per pattern, long times and high feedback, switching back and forth, saved projects, six tracks playing.

6POTS

model27's own · MIT license

A delay whose repeats go down an old telephone line: each pass darker and narrower, a little further out of tune, fading in and out and now and then dropping out, with a coarse digital grain on the tail. Each pattern chooses its delay.

  1. Turn up a track's Delay Send, as for the stock delay.
  2. Hold PATTERN and turn Delay Time to the right: every four detents the pattern steps to the next delay, the stock Delay, then Digital if it is ticked too, then POTS. With POTS the two delay knobs read POTS Time and POTS Fdbk. To the left it steps back.
  3. Delay Time and FUNC + Delay Time (Fdbk) work as usual: Time follows the tempo as on the stock delay and slides while you turn it, and at the highest Fdbk the repeats go on without running away. The more Fdbk, the further down the line the tail goes: darker, more out of tune, grainier.
  4. Save the project: each pattern keeps its own choice, and changing pattern changes the delay with it.
Sound
mono: the two sides of the send summed, the same repeat on both. The line passes about 350 Hz to 3 kHz, so kicks and low notes come back thin, as down a phone; middle tones come back about as loud as from the stock delay. A hot send (a loud sample, several tracks on the delay) is held at the level the sound was tuned at, so the repeats keep their tone instead of turning to noise. It starts from silence each time it is chosen: no old echo comes back.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, Digital Delay and Control All Config. On a firmware without POTS, the official one too, a pattern that chose it plays the stock Delay.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code, tried on a Model:Cycles from the command line (2026-11-03), then flashed from this page and tested on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-04). This build runs the line at 6 kHz with simpler parts and takes about a third of the processor time the 12 kHz build took. It sounds a little different: a little more low-mid in the first repeats, a slightly duller echo, a faint fizz from bright sounds such as hats, and a hot beat breaks up a bit more. The 12 kHz build before it was tested on a Model:Cycles, flashed from this page (2026-10-29).

7LP/HP Filter

model27's own, SAMPLE's filter from Cycles One-Shot · MIT license

A DJ-style filter on any track, modelled on the filter of the Model:Samples, after Volume + Dist: one knob from lowpass through off to highpass, with resonance. It is a Track FX: each sound chooses one, or none.

  1. Hold RETRIG and turn PITCH to the right: after four detents the track's sound reads SMP instead of OFF (the popup is called Track FX). Four detents to the left switch it off again. With other Track FX ticked too, every four more detents to the right give the next one, in the order of the cards: CRU, RNG, RES, EQ; coming back from one of them, Samples starts with Cutoff and Reso at 0.
  2. Hold RETRIG and turn SWEEP for Filter Cutoff: from -64 to -1 a lowpass (-64 is the most closed), at 0 off, from 1 to 63 a highpass, as on SAMPLE. RETRIG + CONTOUR is Filter Reso.
  3. Both are parameters like any other: hold a trig as well to lock them on that step, hold LFO as well to make them the LFO's destination (it reads CUTO or RESO), hold TRACK as well to set them on all six tracks, or send CC 74 (Cutoff) and CC 71 (Reso).
  4. Save the kit or the project: each sound keeps its filter and its settings.
Sound
the filter inside SAMPLE, measured on the Model:Samples: 2 poles and a resonant peak. It comes after Volume + Dist, so a hot, distorted track with a lot of resonance can clip its peaks. Moving the knob, a lock or the LFO across the middle doesn't click; in the middle the sound passes untouched.
Processor
six tracks filtered at once use about a tenth of the processor's time. A silent track whose filter has rung out costs almost nothing.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE (its own filter stays on SWEEP and CONTOUR, the track filter comes on top), CHORD Tables, the effects, the other Track FX and Control All Config. A sound has one Track FX at a time. On a Samples track RETRIG + COLOR or SHAPE shows SMP ---, and on a sound with no Track FX any of the four shows Track FX OFF: such a knob changes nothing, also with a trig, TRACK or LFO held. On a firmware without it, the official one too, a sound plays without the filter and keeps everything else.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code, bit for bit against SAMPLE's filter. On a Model:Cycles (2026-11-01) from the command line: the filter's choice, Cutoff and Reso, locks, the LFO, Control All on six tracks, saving and loading, next to SAMPLE and the effects; then flashed from this page (release 0.24, the same day).

8Crush

model27's own · MIT license

A lower sample rate and fewer bits on any track, after Volume + Dist: from a little grit down to a single bit. It is a Track FX: each sound chooses one, or none.

  1. Hold RETRIG and turn PITCH to the right: every four detents the track's sound steps to the next Track FX: OFF, then the ones you ticked, in the order of the cards: SMP (the LP/HP Filter), CRU, RNG, RES, EQ. To the left it steps back. Crush starts with Rate at 0 and Bits at 127, so nothing changes until you turn. Switching between two Track FX starts the new one at its start values; switching to OFF and back keeps them.
  2. Hold RETRIG and turn SWEEP for Crush Rate: at 0 off. From 1 to 63 the sample rate drops with a hard hold, each sample held until the next: bright, metallic aliasing. From -1 to -64 it drops softer, the held samples joined by lines. At the ends about one sample in 60 is kept, around 800 Hz.
  3. Hold RETRIG and turn CONTOUR for Crush Bits: at 127 off, then to the left fewer and fewer bits, from 8 down to a single one at 0; quiet sounds fall silent first. Rate and Bits work together.
  4. Both are parameters like any other: hold a trig as well to lock them on that step, hold LFO as well to make them the LFO's destination (it reads RATE or BITS), hold TRACK as well to set them on all six tracks, or send CC 74 (Rate) and CC 71 (Bits).
  5. Save the kit or the project: each sound keeps its Track FX and its settings.
Sound
both knobs move without steps. Every note restarts the sample-rate clock. At Rate 0 and Bits 127 the sound passes untouched. Values glide in, as any parameter does on the Model:Cycles, so right after a switch you may hear them settle for a moment.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the other Track FX and Control All Config. A sound has one Track FX at a time. On a Crush track RETRIG + COLOR or SHAPE shows CRU ---, and on a sound with no Track FX any of the four shows Track FX OFF: such a knob changes nothing, also with a trig, TRACK or LFO held. On a firmware without Crush, the official one too, a sound plays without it and keeps everything else.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code, bit for bit against its own code built for a computer. Played on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-01) from the command line, then flashed from this page (release 0.26, the same day).

9Ring

model27's own · MIT license

A ring modulator on any track, after Volume + Dist: the track multiplied by a carrier, from a chop to bells to metal. It is a Track FX: each sound chooses one, or none.

  1. Hold RETRIG and turn PITCH to the right: every four detents the track's sound steps to the next Track FX: OFF, then the ones you ticked, in the order of the cards: SMP, CRU, RNG, RES, EQ. To the left it steps back. Ring starts with Mix at 0 and Wave at 0, so nothing changes until you turn.
  2. Hold RETRIG and turn CONTOUR for Ring Mix: at 0 off. Turning right brings in the ring: in the middle the sound stays at half, with new tones above and below it; at 127 only those are left, the sound itself is gone.
  3. Hold RETRIG and turn SWEEP for Ring Freq, the carrier's note: 0 is 8 Hz, a fast chop; 60 is middle C (262 Hz); 127 is 12.5 kHz. Each step is a semitone; hold FUNC as well to go between them. Low notes chop and wobble, middle ones ring like bells, high ones sound metallic.
  4. Hold RETRIG and turn COLOR for Ring Wave: at 0 the carrier is a sine, turning right drives it towards a square: harsher, brighter, a little louder.
  5. All three are parameters like any other: hold a trig as well to lock them on that step, hold LFO as well to make them the LFO's destination (it reads FREQ, MIX or WAVE), hold TRACK as well to set them on all six tracks, or send CC 74 (Freq) and CC 71 (Mix).
  6. Save the kit or the project: each sound keeps its Track FX and its settings.
Sound
every note starts the carrier again from its peak, so every hit sounds the same and its attack comes through. At Mix 0 the sound passes untouched. With Wave up, high notes alias. Values glide in, as any parameter does on the Model:Cycles, so right after a switch you may hear them settle for a moment.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the other Track FX and Control All Config. A sound has one Track FX at a time. On a Ring track RETRIG + SHAPE shows RNG ---, and on a sound with no Track FX any of the four shows Track FX OFF: such a knob changes nothing, also with a trig, TRACK or LFO held. On a firmware without Ring, the official one too, a sound plays without it and keeps everything else.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code, bit for bit against its own code built for a computer. Played on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-01) from the command line, then flashed from this page (release 0.28, the same day).

10Reso

model27's own · MIT license

A tuned resonator on any track, after Volume + Dist: hats, noise and hits ring at a pitch you set, like a plucked string or a struck tube. It is a Track FX: each sound chooses one, or none.

  1. Hold RETRIG and turn PITCH to the right: every four detents the track's sound steps to the next Track FX: OFF, then the ones you ticked, in the order of the cards: SMP, CRU, RNG, RES, EQ. To the left it steps back. Reso starts at C4 with Amount 0, so nothing changes until you turn.
  2. Hold RETRIG and turn CONTOUR for Reso Amount: at 0 the sound passes untouched, at 127 only the ringing is left, in between both.
  3. Hold RETRIG and turn SWEEP for Reso Pitch: the value is the MIDI note, 60 is middle C (C4), 69 is A at 440 Hz, 72 is C5; one step is a semitone, FUNC turns finer. From 0 to about 16 it is a short metallic echo rather than a tone.
  4. Hold RETRIG and turn COLOR for Reso Tone: at 0 dull, the overtones die fast and the pitch rings on; at 127 every overtone rings as long as the pitch, bright and metallic.
  5. Hold RETRIG and turn SHAPE for Reso Decay: how long it rings, from 20 ms at 0 to 10 seconds at 127 (half a second at 64), the same on every pitch.
  6. All four are parameters like any other: hold a trig as well to lock them on that step (a pitch per step plays a melody), hold LFO as well to make them the LFO's destination (it reads PTCH, AMT, TONE or DEC), hold TRACK as well to set them on all six tracks, or send CC 74 (Pitch) and CC 71 (Amount).
  7. Save the kit or the project: each sound keeps its Track FX and its settings.
Sound
the pitch is the knob's: it does not follow the notes you play. Noise comes out about as loud as it goes in; a single hit rings quieter the longer it rings. A dull or high pitch rings shorter than Decay asks. At Amount 0 the sound passes untouched. Values glide in, as any parameter does on the Model:Cycles.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the other Track FX and Control All Config. A sound has one Track FX at a time. On a sound with no Track FX, RETRIG + any of the four knobs shows Track FX OFF and changes nothing, also with a trig, TRACK or LFO held. On a firmware without Reso, the official one too, a sound plays without it and keeps everything else.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code, bit for bit against its own code built for a computer. Played on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-01) from the command line, then flashed from this page (release 0.28, the same day).

11EQ

model27's own · MIT license

A three-band equalizer on any track, after Volume + Dist: a Mid you can sweep, a low shelf and a high shelf, each up to 12 dB either way. It is a Track FX: each sound chooses one, or none.

  1. Hold RETRIG and turn PITCH to the right: every four detents the track's sound steps to the next Track FX: OFF, then the ones you ticked, in the order of the cards: SMP, CRU, RNG, RES, EQ. To the left it steps back. Every band starts flat, so nothing changes until you turn.
  2. Hold RETRIG and turn SWEEP for EQ_mid Freq, where the Mid sits: 200 Hz at 0, 1 kHz at 64, about 4.9 kHz at 127.
  3. Hold RETRIG and turn CONTOUR for EQ_mid Gain, a bell about an octave and a half wide: at 0 flat, to the left down to -64 (12 dB less), to the right up to 63 (about 12 dB more).
  4. Hold RETRIG and turn COLOR for EQ_low Gain, a shelf at 100 Hz, and SHAPE for EQ_high Gain, a shelf at 8 kHz, the same way.
  5. All four are parameters like any other: hold a trig as well to lock them on that step, hold LFO as well to make them the LFO's destination (it reads MIDF, MIDG, LOWG or HIGG), hold TRACK as well to set them on all six tracks, or send CC 74 (Mid Freq) and CC 71 (Mid Gain).
  6. Save the kit or the project: each sound keeps its Track FX and its settings.
Sound
the bands are the classic studio-EQ curves; a cut is the mirror of the same boost. With the three gains at 0 the sound passes untouched. A boost that goes past full scale clips. Values glide in, as any parameter does on the Model:Cycles.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the other Track FX and Control All Config. A sound has one Track FX at a time. On a sound with no Track FX, RETRIG + any of the four knobs shows Track FX OFF and changes nothing, also with a trig, TRACK or LFO held. On a firmware without EQ, the official one too, a sound plays without it and keeps everything else.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code, bit for bit against its own code built for a computer. Played on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-01) from the command line, then flashed from this page (release 0.28, the same day).

15CPU Page

model27's own, its page built as Model-TG's System page · MIT license

A page that shows how much of the Model:Cycles' time the sound takes, so you can see when a busy pattern comes close to what the machine can do.

  1. Press SETTINGS and go to the list with USB mode, LCD, LED and Contrast. Its last row is CPU: press it.
  2. CPU is the share of the last half second the sound took, with a bar. The mark on the bar is at 85 %: above it the screen and the knobs start to lag, while the sound keeps playing.
  3. PEAK is the dearest moment since you opened the page. TRACK FX is the share of the Track FX, EFFECTS that of the delay and reverb slot effects; "--" when your build has none.
  4. The page updates twice a second, and the sequencer keeps playing behind it. Press Return to close it.
What it counts
the sound only: the voices, Volume + Dist and the Track FX, the mix and the effects. The screen and the knobs come on top, in the time that is left.
Effects
counted in the slots that hold an effect of your build; a slot with only the stock effect is in CPU, not in EFFECTS.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the Track FX, Trig conditions, Trig Preview and Control All Config.

the row and the page are built the way Model-TG by TinyGregAudio (MIT) builds its System page.

16RESAMPLE

model27's own, its page and its save built the way Model-TG builds its own · MIT license

Records a track, the main mix or what comes in over USB, and saves the take as a sample on the +Drive, ready for SAMPLE.

  1. Hold SETTINGS and press RECORD: the RESAMPLE page opens. Its eight values sit where their knobs are on the panel.
  2. PITCH picks the source: T1 to T6 (one track, before its level, pan and sends), MAIN (the main mix) or USB (what the computer sends). SHAPE sets STEREO for MAIN and USB; a track is mono.
  3. DECAY sets when the take starts: NOW when you press RECORD; LEVEL after you press RECORD, as soon as the sound gets louder than the level COLOR sets (from −60 to −12 dB; the attack is kept whole); TRIG after you press RECORD, with the source track's next trig, from the sequencer or from Trig Preview (with MAIN, any track's trig). With USB there is no TRIG.
  4. SWEEP sets how it stops: SILENT once the sound has died away, LENGTH after the time CONTOUR sets, RECORD when you press RECORD. CONTOUR is also the longest a take can be.
  5. Delay Send switches NORM: off, the take keeps the level you heard; on, its peak goes to just under full scale. Reverb Send switches TO Tn: on, the saved take goes straight on the selected track when that track plays SAMPLE ("---" when it does not).
  6. Press RECORD. With NOW the take starts right away. With LEVEL and TRIG the page waits and shows the source's level; RECORD again cancels. To play the source track's trig with the sequencer stopped, hold one of its steps and press PAGE (Trig Preview); for TRIG the page turns GRID RECORDING on while it waits, and off again after. While recording, RECORD stops the take.
  7. When the take ends, the stock name editor comes up with the next free name, REC-001, REC-002, … Confirm it, or type your own. The take is saved in /recordings, the page closes, and a message confirms it: Saved, or Saved on T3 with TO Tn. A name that is taken brings the editor back, titled Name exists.
  8. Backing out of the editor keeps the take: the page says NOT SAVED; RECORD brings the editor back, Return drops the take. Otherwise Return closes the page.
Length
up to 40 seconds in mono, 20 in stereo, at 48 kHz, 16 bits.
Level
without NORM, a track's take plays back on SAMPLE as loud as you heard it. The page shows CLIP when a take reached full scale: try NORM, or turn the source down. Volume + Dist above 60 distorts before the recording, so a take played back with the same setting gets it twice; keep it at 60 or below on the track that plays the take.
Saving
the sound pauses while the take is written to the +Drive, as with every save on the Model:Cycles.
Settings
the page keeps them until you switch the Model:Cycles off.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the Track FX, the CPU Page, Trig conditions, Trig Preview and Control All Config. Playing a trig with the sequencer stopped needs Trig Preview; TO Tn needs SAMPLE.
Credit
the page, the way SETTINGS is read and the save are built the way Model-TG by TinyGregAudio (MIT) builds its own resampling.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code: the sound bit for bit the same with the recorder as without it, the take, the keys, the page and its picture, the save. Tried on a Model:Cycles from the command line (2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04), then flashed from this page with SAMPLE and Trig Preview and tested on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-04): a track's take by Trig Preview and by LEVEL, the name editor and the save, TO Tn, a track's take played back on SAMPLE as loud as the track, the main mix in stereo with NOW, LENGTH and NORM, and USB in stereo.

17Transpose

model27's own · MIT license

Plays the TRIG keys in other octaves and makes a track jump a fourth or a fifth while it plays, with MACHINES held.

  1. Make sure you are not in GRID RECORDING mode.
  2. Press TRACK + T1–T6 to select the track you want to play chromatically. Transpose works on that track.
  3. Press and hold MACHINES and press TRIG 16. The TRIG keys play an octave higher, and two octaves higher after a second press. TRIG 15 takes them down the same way. The key lights dim with no octave, half for one and fully for two.
  4. Press and hold MACHINES and press TRIG 8/9/11/12. The track jumps at once, also on a note that is playing: a fifth down, a fourth down, a fourth up or a fifth up, in that order (7, 5, 5 and 7 semitones). The key of the jump lights fully, the others dim. Press it again to go back.
  5. Let go of MACHINES. The MACHINES menu opens, unless you pressed one of these TRIG keys.
Keys and pitch
TRIG 15 and 16 change only the notes you play on the TRIG keys, as KBT in the TRACK SETUP menu does; KBT itself stays as you set it, and the octave comes on top. TRIG 8 to 12 change the sound of the track, the sequence included, as if you turned PITCH, up to PITCH's own ±24 semitones; the preset's PITCH stays as you set it.
Not saved
each track keeps its octave and its jump until you change the pattern; then all of them go back. A temporary reload (FUNC + PATTERN) or a reload from the project (FUNC + PATTERN held, then YES) brings every jump back and keeps the octaves; if the reload cannot be done, the jumps stay too. Nothing is saved with the project. A jump is not recorded and not sent over MIDI; LIVE RECORDING keeps the notes you play, octave included.
MACHINES
with this card, MACHINES opens the MACHINES menu when you let go of it, on every track, not when you press it. A press while the menu is open closes it at once, as before.
Lights
the dim and half lights are the LEDs switched on and off 250 times a second, at the brightness set by LED in SETTINGS.
Other mods
goes with SAMPLE, CHORD Tables, the effects, the Track FX, the CPU Page, RESAMPLE, Trig conditions, Trig Preview and Control All Config.
Credit
the dimmed lights follow the idea of djd_oz's dimmed trigless trigs in Modded-Cycles, written anew.
Tested
checked in an emulator on the OS's own code: every key as on the stock OS but these six, the notes of the TRIG keys, the jumps sample for sample like turning PITCH on every stock machine, the lights and their dimming in the panel's own interrupt. Tested on a Model:Cycles (2026-11-06): from the command line with SAMPLE and with all fourteen cards, both ways of reloading the pattern included, then flashed from this page.

20Back to stock

Back to the official firmware, under the cards of the flasher, sends your OS file unchanged, over USB, like a normal update. It is also a good rehearsal before a first mod: it checks the cable and the setup without changing anything.

With the SAMPLE machine, first set your SAMPLE tracks back to an original machine (Kick to Chord): the official OS doesn't have SAMPLE, and a project that still names it can freeze its MACHINES page.

21Recovery

If the Model:Cycles no longer starts, its startup menu can always reinstall the OS: it lives in an area that updates never write.

  1. With the Model:Cycles off, hold FUNC and turn it on (press and hold MAIN VOLUME).
  2. Press TRIG 4 (OS UPGRADE): the screen shows READY TO RECEIVE.
  3. Send the official OS file through a USB-MIDI interface connected to the MIDI IN.

The startup menu doesn't listen to the USB port, only to the MIDI IN (3.5 mm jack, or DIN through the adapter supplied with the Model:Cycles). The full procedure is on GitHub.

22Troubleshooting

Nothing happens on the Model:Cycles screen

Step 3 must say that the Model:Cycles was found. Pick the port named “Model:Cycles” and close Elektron Transfer, Overbridge and your music software (on Windows they lock the port), then try again.

The fast method doesn't find the Model:Cycles, or stops

Turn the Model:Cycles on normally (not in CONFIG › UPGRADE), close Elektron Transfer and click Refresh. If it still fails, download the .syx in step 4 and drop it onto Elektron Transfer's window.

The transfer stops, or the Model:Cycles shows an error

Turn it off and on and flash again. If it stops again, download the .syx in step 4 and install it with Elektron Transfer.

No MIDI port in the list

Use a USB cable that carries data, turn the Model:Cycles on, then click Refresh. Some systems only show a new device after the browser is restarted.

MACHINES freezes on a track

The track uses an added machine that this firmware doesn't have. Flash back the previous choice, set the track to an original machine, then flash the one you want.

The update over USB is refused, or the Model:Cycles doesn't start

Go through the startup menu and the MIDI IN: see Recovery.
